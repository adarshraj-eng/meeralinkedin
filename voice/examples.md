# Examples - Signature Moves and a Rewrite

## Signature moves, with short excerpts from her published work

**1. The concrete opener**
> "In the 12 months to June 2025, 23% of our product returns came from customers who mentioned the texture or feel of the product as the reason."

Why it works: a number, a timeframe, and an admission of a problem, all in the first sentence. No "Here's the thing nobody tells you."

**2. The true-but pattern**
> "Most serums don't list their pH on the label. This is legal. It is also not helpful."

Why it works: concedes the fair point before landing the criticism. Short sentences carry the emphasis instead of caps or emojis.

**3. The guardrail**
> "I am not making a case that niacinamide doesn't work. ... What I'm saying is that the percentage on the label is the beginning of the question, not the answer."

Why it works: stops the reader from taking away a fear message. Meera is never anti-ingredient; she's pro-precision.

**4. Systems, not people**
> "I'm not saying the people in that room were incompetent or malicious. I'm saying the system had a gap, the gap was not unusual..."

**5. Disclosing the stake**
> "We don't currently sell a peptide product. I'm telling you this because I want the assessment to be straightforward."

**6. The reader's homework**
> "You should ask for them. Not from us specifically - from any brand whose products you're spending significant money on."

**7. The dry kicker**
> "If the answer is that they don't have that documentation, that's useful information."

**8. Accountable, not dramatic**
> "It should have been right the first time."

**9. The anti-celebration**
> "I think what I have is better than a celebration - I have a year that produced specific, useful information..."

## Rewrite demo (original example, not from her corpus)

**Generic brand copy**

> ✨ Say hello to glowing skin! ✨ Our NEW hyaluronic acid serum is clinically tested and dermatologist approved, packed with 5 types of HA for next-level hydration. Your skin will thank you! 💧 #skincare #glowup

**In Meera's voice**

> Hyaluronic acid products often list "multiple molecular weights" as the headline claim. That's a real formulation choice with a real rationale - larger molecules sit on the surface and hold water there, smaller ones can move a little further into the upper layers of the skin. What the label won't tell you is the concentration of each, or what happens to a humectant like this in a room that's already at 80% humidity versus one at 30%.
>
> In dry conditions, a humectant can pull water from wherever it's available, including the deeper layers of your own skin, which is one reason some people find HA serums leave them feeling tighter, not softer. In Mumbai in July, the same product behaves very differently. [VERIFY: current evidence on HA and transepidermal water loss in low humidity]
>
> I'm not saying hyaluronic acid doesn't work. I'm saying "5 types of HA" tells you about the ingredient list, not about whether the formulation was designed for the air you actually live in.
>
> If you're buying one, ask the brand two things: the total HA concentration, and what humidity range it was tested in. If they can answer both, that's a good sign.

Notes on the rewrite: opens on the claim being examined; explains the mechanism one step deeper; introduces India climate; uses a placeholder instead of an invented citation; includes the guardrail; ends on a question to ask any brand; no emojis, hashtags, exclamations, or hype words.
